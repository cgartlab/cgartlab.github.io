# How to Achieve Catastrophe Through Force

When you are alone and no one else comes to mind, you are happy and feel no loneliness.

- When a memory is triggered — people and events from the past surface — your body is still alone, but your mind is already in a relationship.
- If that person or event doesn't respond to you (insufficient feedback), or doesn't bring a sense of safety, loneliness sets in. It often comes with fear, self-doubt, self-denial, self-blame, and all kinds of internal friction. That feeling is always terrible.
- In this state, if no other thought interrupts you, you fall into a hallucination of past trauma.
- People feel lonely precisely because they have or have had toxic relationships. Their mind stays stuck inside those relationships.
- Avoiding relationships deliberately is often because the bodily and psychological memories triggered by unresponsive or ineffective interactions are unbearable and painful. Interaction stirs up those painful memories. Without relationships, those memories stay buried in the subconscious.
- If someone is highly capable and enjoys being alone, but carries a history of hurtful, unfulfilling experiences, and no one is willing to help them heal (people only approach for their abilities and refuse to fill the void), it's normal that they don't want to build new relationships.
- Even when interacting with the same person who feels no negativity, they still feel terrible. Because they're not just facing the person in front of them — they're facing a mountain of bad memories.
- Refusing to build relationships ultimately leads back to the starting point: building a relationship with oneself. A spring compressed to its limit, when released, will snap back to its full extension, unleashing energy. Use that energy well, and it can propel you forward and upward. Misuse it, and it breeds destruction and danger.
- Great force creates miracles — or catastrophes.
- They already know they are "broken" and cannot properly build relationships or handle situations similar to their trauma. Every time the trauma is triggered, it's like a full moon turning a werewolf — a passive reflex.
- Closing off the heart is the greatest protection they can offer both themselves and those around them. At its core, it's an awareness of how destructive the trauma is, and an active effort to prevent it from spreading like a virus.
- This is also why most people who suffered trauma in their original families decide not to have children. They don't have enough ability, opportunity, or time to fully resolve the harm their environment caused them — yet they still possess enough self-reflection and sense of responsibility.
- The shallow and selfish side will blame the other person for not building a relationship, for not responding. They haven't realized that in a relationship, all you need to do is provide what the other needs. If you can't provide it, and you refuse to face your own inadequacy, instead blaming and attacking — aren't you just recreating the same traumatic environment they once knew?
- A truly strong person understands: even with unlimited power and resources, coercion only gets you a slave.
