The cover shows a Colorful iGame Water God 3070 that was sent back for repair after the Chinese New Year. Probably pushed a bit too hard, either the fan or the water pump started making a "tick-tick" noise under low load. Since this card is my bread and butter, I was worried it might bring the whole system down, so I contacted JD.com customer service to send it back to the manufacturer for repair.

The whole process was fairly pleasant. When it came back, not only was the noise issue fixed, but they also applied a protective film and repackaged it. The most outrageous part was that they threw in another pair of white gloves (they gave me a pair when I first bought it too). After reinstalling and testing for about a week, everything worked normally — safe and sound. I'm mentioning this because I came across the cool tech below 👇.

## A New GPU Renderer — Centileo Render

An unbiased GPU renderer made by our Russian friends, said to be targeting Octane and Redshift. It supports 3ds Max and Cinema 4D, and is currently in free public beta. According to their website, this renderer's biggest advantages are speed, noise control, and high throughput for large scene data.

I gave it a test, and the speed is stunning. For the photo-realistic quality shown below, the parameters were actually set very low, and my 3070 finished it in just 4 seconds! The only minor shortcoming is that it doesn't yet support the Pyro fluid simulation system, so it can't render volumetric light or fog effects. However, it's already quite usable for producing regular effect renders.

## Microsoft Store Integrates App Treasure

I normally don't use this store — I just happened to open it by accident. It might be because I joined Windows Insider, so I received the push early. At first I thought Microsoft was expanding the variety of apps in the store, or making it easier to tap into the "Simplified Chinese internet", but on closer inspection, those banners are all mobile games, aren't they? Holy cow — did they actually build an Android emulator right in?

Tried it out — and yes, that's exactly what it is. Currently there are relatively few apps. I imagine the use case might be for people who are too lazy to pick up their phones, or who need to run multiple accounts. Nothing that feels particularly essential.

## Some New Discoveries About Obsidian

In Obsidian's update on February 18, 2025, a new official plugin called **Web Viewer** was added. This feature is similar to web clipping tools, allowing users to view and embed web content directly within Obsidian.

For desktop clients, stuffing a browser into an app is something I've always disliked — desktop WeChat is a prime example. When there's already a browser, why cram another one in?

Also, Obsidian paired with [Git sync](https://github.com/ViscousPot/GitSync) finally works smoothly on Android. This tool focuses exclusively on Git sync, has a very friendly interface, and can even leverage the phone's accessibility screen recognition for automatic sync. Happy about this.
