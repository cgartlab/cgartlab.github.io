## Worthwhile New Updates

### PNG 3 Update

![PNG 3 format features overview showing APNG animation, HDR color, and Exif metadata support](../_images/07%20玄光周刊-双钻石设计模型：从用户需求到产品落地的黄金框架解析-1754597038333.webp)

The PNG format is something almost every designer, illustrator, and artist encounters on a regular basis. Last month, the latest PNG 3 specification was released. PNG (**Portable Network Graphics**) is a widely used **lossless compression** bitmap image format, designed as a replacement for the earlier GIF format. That's right—the original GIF was also a widely used static image format, but thanks to its animation support, it eventually became synonymous with animated images and memes.

For designers, PNG 3 brings a few new features worth attention:

- **Official APNG Animation Support (Replacing GIF)**
  Higher-quality animated images: APNG supports 24-bit true color (16 million colors) and 8-bit alpha transparency, compared to GIF's 256 colors and 1-bit transparency—delivering smoother gradients and anti-aliasing.
  Smaller file sizes: APNG uses intelligent inter-frame compression, storing only the changed parts, which is more space-efficient than GIF.
  Broader compatibility: Previously, APNG was only partially supported by browsers (Chrome, Firefox, Safari); now it's an official standard, and broader software support is expected going forward.
  Design applications: Suitable for high-quality animated logos, UI micro-interactions, memes, and more—can replace GIF.

- **Native HDR Support (High Dynamic Range)**
  Wider color gamut & higher brightness: PNG 3 supports HDR color through CICP (just 16 bytes), suitable for professional photography, gaming, and film/TV scenarios that demand high-fidelity color.

- **Native Exif Metadata Support**
  Better copyright management: Embeds shooting parameters, copyright info, GPS coordinates, and more—making image provenance and management easier.
  Optimized storage format: Compared to older unofficial Exif embedding, the new version is more efficient and reduces redundant data.

- **Lighter Color Space Description (CICP)**
  Reduced file size: Compared to traditional ICC color profiles (300+ bytes), CICP requires only 16 bytes—ideal for large quantities of small images (such as web icons).

### Double Diamond Design Philosophy

![Double Diamond design model diagram showing the four stages: discover, define, develop, deliver](../_images/07%20玄光周刊-双钻石设计模型：从用户需求到产品落地的黄金框架解析-1754597085815.webp)

The Double Diamond design philosophy is an innovation methodology widely used in product development, brand design, and other fields. I mentioned it in [The Art of Dream-Making: Behind the Scenes of Film Production and the Innovation of AI Tools](https://cgartlab.com/posts/weekly-05/)—I've rewatched that video several times. The author's overall framework for building a brand is an extension of this philosophy, and it's well worth studying. Highly recommended. This philosophy very clearly and precisely divides the design process into four stages:

#### **1. Discover**

- **Core Task**: This stage is primarily about understanding the problem. In product development, for example, you need to develop a deep understanding of user needs—such as their expectations for product features and usage scenarios. You also need to pay attention to market demand, including the competitive landscape. At the same time, you need to clarify the challenges at hand—for instance, whether you have the capability to develop a new product that meets those needs.
- **Output**: A clear picture of user needs, market conditions, and challenges—laying the foundation for subsequent stages.

#### **2. Define**

- **Core Task**: Determine the design challenge. The team consolidates the insights and pain points gathered during Discover, analyzes existing solutions on the market, and identifies unmet needs or opportunities for improvement.
- **Output**: A clear design challenge statement that helps focus the direction of subsequent design work.

#### **3. Develop**

- **Core Task**: Explore potential solutions. Return to the original perspective and way of thinking—for example, by sketching to capture and share ideas, while conducting supporting research. For promising ideas, build product prototypes quickly.
- **Output**: Product prototypes—an initial physical representation of the design concept that can be used for testing and further refinement.

#### **4. Deliver**

- **Core Task**: Build an effective solution. Iterate prototypes rapidly to keep costs down, taking materials, manufacturing processes, and other factors into account. At the same time, work on product naming, messaging, and marketing briefs—including following naming conventions, communicating the product's core narrative, and writing marketing copy.
- **Output**: The final product or service, along with the associated brand promotion strategy.

The strength of the Double Diamond design philosophy lies in its emphasis on iteration—every stage can be adjusted based on feedback—and its consistent user-centered orientation. Applicable across many fields such as product development and brand design, it helps teams move systematically from idea to actual product or service delivery.

## What Are Stylescapes?

![Example Stylescape — a multidimensional visual direction board embedding design into real scenarios](../_images/07%20玄光周刊-双钻石设计模型：从用户需求到产品落地的黄金框架解析-1754597111214.webp)

In any visual design process, designers gather visual references and arrange them together by color, style, and other criteria—a collection called a moodboard. Today I came across a new concept from abroad: **Stylescapes**, which can be literally translated as "风格景观" (style landscapes).

Their shared purpose is to align the team's understanding of visual direction, serving as a source of inspiration and a decision-making tool in the early stages of design.

The biggest differences between the two come down to this: moodboards are static and tend to focus on a single dimension of visual information—such as color, material, or thematic style—without connecting to concrete usage scenarios. Stylescapes, on the other hand, are multidimensional, embedding stylistic elements into real usage scenarios (such as cafés, product packaging, event materials, and so on).

It's not hard to see that moodboards have their limits: they only cover the early stages of a project. Once a specific visual style is locked in and you move into production, the cost of making changes later becomes very high. Stylescapes run through the entire design cycle—from concept design to product development and brand communication—providing a unified visual language framework. They also support horizontal expansion across digital interfaces, physical products, and offline events, keeping the brand style consistent throughout.

Stepping outside the project workflow and looking from the designer's perspective: moodboards tend to be designer-led, relying heavily on the designer's intuition, experience, and aesthetic judgment—they emphasize _feel_. Stylescapes are user-centered, using scenario simulation and user research to analyze real feedback from target audiences in different contexts. They focus more on how visual styles are _perceived and used_.
